# Direction (agreed Nov 2026)

## The pitch
You come down on a derelict colony planet. The colonists never left: they're still here, changed. Something on this
world is watching you through things: the planet's eye. You need parts to get off-world, and the parts are where the
truth is. Learning it changes how it ends. One life per character; the world remembers.

## Pillars
- **Horror:** psychological + folk + cosmic wrongness. Not RimWorld: nothing cosy, nothing clean.
- **Threats:** the stalker (the planet's eye), twisted creatures, changed colonists, and the land itself.
- **Look:** dark but still weird. Keep the strange flora, but darker and sicker: the colour itself feels diseased.
- **Buildings:** mostly shanty: corrugated iron, scrap timber, tarps, hull plates, leaning, gaps, dirt floors.
  Rotting landing-era prefab blocks too. Heavy decay, and signs of what happened in every building.
  Large towns exist, but deeper in (prefab colony towns, not 70s Essex: the estates are dropped).
- **Sanity that lies:** low sanity shows you fake things: phantom creatures, a wrong map, whispers, items that aren't there.
- **People:** changed colonists. Some talk (broken, unsettling); you can trade, hide, fight, or be taken in.
- **Progression:** outskirts to the heart. Quiet shanty fringes first; inward = bigger towns, worse wrongness, the truth.
- **Goal:** escape and mystery together. Ship parts are where the story is; the truth changes the ending.
- **Death:** permadeath, the world persists.
- **Sound:** near silence (wind, creaks, footsteps, distant things), detuned drones that swell with danger or madness,
  broken old music (warped records, a far radio in an empty shack), FM effects kept but darker.
- **Keep:** character creation + archetypes (incl. the Drift crew), real-time movement, Zomboid-style looting,
  the title card.
- **Order of work:** look first, then systems.

## Timeline (milestones, in order)

### 1. The look: make it creepy (first)
1. Palette + light: darker, sicker flora; heavier dark; drifting fog; desaturated ground; lamps and fire are precious.
2. Shanty settlements replace the 70s estates: hand-built shacks of iron, timber, tarp and hull plate, leaning,
   gaps, dirt floors, lean-tos, washing lines, fire barrels, tracks rather than streets.
3. Decay for every building: holes, collapsed walls, caved floors, debris, overturned and broken furniture, stains,
   growth through floors; "how far gone" varies by place.
4. What happened: barricaded doors, tally marks, kitchen shrines, beds pushed against doors, a chair facing a wall,
   notes (short, unsettling, finding them is part of the mystery).
5. Landing-era prefab blocks: the colony's original units, rotting and overgrown, at the edges of shanties.
6. Sound pass: near silence, creaks, a detuned drone layer, warped radios/records in some shacks, darker FM.

### 2. The world's shape
Rings from outskirts to the heart: wrongness and danger rise inward; prefab towns deeper in; the land itself
(fog banks, sinkholes, places that shift when unseen).

### 3. Sanity that lies
Sanity meter (dark, the eye, colonists, what you find; rest, light and food restore it). Low sanity: phantoms,
whispers, the minimap and map lie, fake loot, subtle screen distortion (reusing the trip effects, gently).

### 4. The eye (the stalker)
The planet watching you through things: building from signs, to glimpses at the edge of the light, to being seen.
Grows the longer and deeper you go. Ties into the existing eye event.

### 5. Changed colonists
Behaviours (watch, follow, hide, hunt), stealth (crouch, hide in shacks, noise), broken speech, trade, rumours, lies,
recruitment ("join us"), and fighting as a costly last resort.

### 6. Goal and mystery
Ship parts placed at story places; notes and visions that piece together what happened; endings (escape / join /
the truth).

### 7. Permadeath and runs
One life; the world persists; your past characters' graves and belongings are found by the next.
